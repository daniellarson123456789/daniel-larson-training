Sales associate and REALTOR®: what’s the difference?

The Florida license is issued by the state. REALTOR® membership is a separate professional choice.

The distinction

Florida issues a sales associate license through DBPR. REALTOR® is a membership term for licensed real estate professionals who belong to the National Association of REALTORS®. The school course and state exam lead toward a license; association membership is a separate step.

What the state license does

A Florida sales associate applies to DBPR, completes the approved prelicense education, passes the state licensing exam, and has the license activated under an employing broker before practicing. See the full sales associate path. The state credential is also distinct from a broker license, which has additional experience and education requirements.

What REALTOR® means

The National Association of REALTORS® says a REALTOR® is a licensed real estate professional who is also an NAR member. Members join through a local association and agree to the association’s Code of Ethics. The word is a membership mark, rather than another name for the Florida sales associate license.

You can earn a state license without joining NAR. If you later choose association membership, follow the local association’s membership process. Read NAR’s explanation.

What happens to your license, the hardship option, and how sales associates and brokers get back to work.

The short answer

If your first renewal date has not passed yet, finish Post-Licensing now. If it has passed without Post-Licensing, the license is null and void. A sales associate requalifies by completing Course 1 again and passing the state exam. A documented hardship may support a request to the Florida Real Estate Commission (FREC) for up to six more months.

Post-Licensing is the education for your first renewal: 45 hours for sales associates and 60 hours for brokers. Ordinary 14-hour CE does not replace it. See which renewal course applies to you.

First, check whether the deadline has passed.

Look up your license status and expiration date in your DBPR online account. First renewals fall on March 31 or September 30. If your date is still ahead of you, you have not missed anything yet.

Still before the deadline? Enroll in Post-Licensing and finish it before the expiration date on your license. After you pass, we report your completion to DBPR. Then complete the separate license renewal. See sales and broker Post-Licensing.

Not sure of your dates? Estimate your renewal timeline, then confirm it in DBPR.

What happens when Post-Licensing is missed?

Florida does not treat missed Post-Licensing like a late CE renewal. A sales associate or broker who does not complete the required Post-Licensing before the first renewal has a license that is null and void. It does not move into an inactive status you can reactivate with a later course.

That means you cannot practice on that license. Talk with your broker right away about any pending transactions.

We run into this often, and we know the process for getting you back to work, even after a catastrophic missed course deadline. Contact us as soon as you can after a missed deadline — text Daniel at 239.471.8500.

A four-year degree in real estate from an accredited institution exempts a licensee from Post-Licensing. If that applies to you, confirm it with DBPR.

Is there a hardship extension?

FREC may allow an additional six months after the first renewal to complete Post-Licensing when a licensee could not finish because of an individual hardship. The kinds of hardship FREC recognizes include:

  • a long-term illness, or the illness of a close relative or someone you care for;
  • the required course not being reasonably available; or
  • an economic or technological hardship that substantially affected your ability to complete the education.

The request goes to FREC in writing and explains the hardship. FREC may ask for supporting documents and decides whether to grant it. A school cannot grant an extension. We can help you understand what to include. See how we help with hardship situations.

Sales associates: how to requalify.

Without an approved extension, a sales associate whose license became null and void must requalify: complete the 63-hour sales associate prelicense course (Course 1) again and pass the state exam again. Then the new license goes through Post-Licensing at its own first renewal.

Brokers: two ways back.

A broker who misses the 60-hour broker Post-Licensing also has a null and void license, with two options:

  • Return as a sales associate. A sales associate license may be issued after you show that you completed the 14-hour continuing education course within the six months following the expiration of your broker license. See 14-hour CE.
  • Return as a broker. Requalify by completing the broker prelicense course (Course 2) again and passing the broker exam again. See the broker requirements.

Confirm which option fits your record with DBPR before you enroll.

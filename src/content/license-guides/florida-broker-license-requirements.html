Broker licensing

How to get a Florida broker license

Qualifying experience, the 72-hour Course 2, Broker FastPass, and the separate state exam.

The short answer

Qualifying broker experience generally means 24 active months within the preceding five years. Apply to DBPR, complete the approved 72-hour broker prelicense course and certification, then pass the separate broker state exam.

What does “two of the last five years” mean?

Florida law recognizes an active sales associate license for at least 24 months within the preceding five years under one or more brokers in Florida or another qualifying jurisdiction. It also describes certain government-agency experience and an out-of-state broker-license path. Merely holding an inactive license for two calendar years does not describe the usual sales-associate path.

If you claim experience from another state or jurisdiction, DBPR requests a current certification of license history documenting it. A person licensed as a Florida sales associate in the preceding five years must also complete the applicable sales post-licensing education before broker licensure. Check DBPR’s Florida sales-associate-to-broker checklist, its out-of-state experience checklist, and Florida Statutes §475.17 for the qualifying paths.

Your broker path

Course 2 and Broker FastPass

Florida’s standard broker prelicense course is 72 hours. Daniel Larson Training offers Course 2 as a self-paced course, with a separate student login. With Broker FastPass, you can start the three broker exam-style certification exams when you’re ready, using Course 2, 500+ Q&A, audio, video, and the FastPass book as your preparation tools. The Florida broker licensing exam is still a separate Pearson test. See current broker course options.

If you already have a valid broker prelicense certificate from elsewhere, Broker Exam Prep Only is the separate preparation option.

Approval and Pearson

Apply while you study if that timing helps. Once DBPR approves your broker application and you are exam eligible, use the authorization details to schedule the Florida Broker exam with Pearson. The official Florida Real Estate program page serves both sales and broker candidates; select Broker during scheduling.

Broker test-day checklist

  • Print and bring the valid Course 2 prelicensing completion certificate on every exam attempt.
  • Bring two valid forms of signature ID, one government issued.

The broker candidate booklet says an expired certificate will not be accepted even if exam authorization is still valid. Read the certificate and exam guide.

Course access and state deadlines

Daniel Larson Training’s broker course bundles provide one year of online access; that is the school’s access term. Under Florida Statutes §475.181(2), your broker prelicense course completion counts for licensing for two years from completion, and your application expires two years after DBPR receives it if you have not passed the exam. DBPR says exam authorization lasts two years from approval. Each date belongs to a different step.

After the broker exam

A new Florida broker’s first renewal generally requires 60 hours of broker Post-Licensing; later renewals generally use 14-hour CE. Compare Post and CE.

How your course fits the licensing process

Qualifying broker experience generally means 24 active months within the preceding five years. Apply to DBPR, complete the approved 72-hour broker prelicense course and certification, then pass the separate broker state exam.

What does “two of the last five years” mean?

The usual path is an active sales associate license for at least 24 months within the preceding five years, under one or more brokers in Florida or another qualifying jurisdiction. Holding an inactive license for two calendar years does not count the same way. If you held a Florida sales associate license in the preceding five years, finish the applicable sales Post-Licensing before broker licensure.

Other routes, such as out-of-state experience or certain government employment, have their own documents. See how to count qualifying experience, then check DBPR’s Florida sales-associate-to-broker checklist or its out-of-state experience checklist.

Approval and Pearson

Apply while you study if that timing helps. Once DBPR approves your broker application and you are exam eligible, use the authorization details to schedule the Florida Broker exam with Pearson. The official Florida Real Estate program page serves both sales and broker candidates; select Broker during scheduling.

Broker test-day checklist

  • Print and bring the valid Course 2 prelicensing completion certificate on every exam attempt.
  • Bring two valid forms of signature ID, one government issued.

The broker candidate booklet says an expired certificate will not be accepted even if exam authorization is still valid. Read the certificate and exam guide.

Course access and state deadlines

Daniel Larson Training’s broker course bundles provide one year of online access; that is the school’s access term. Under Florida law, your broker prelicense course completion counts for licensing for two years from completion, and your application expires two years after DBPR receives it if you have not passed the exam. DBPR says exam authorization lasts two years from approval. Each date belongs to a different step. See the dates side by side.

After the broker exam

A new Florida broker’s first renewal generally requires 60 hours of broker Post-Licensing; later renewals generally use 14-hour CE. Compare Post and CE and estimate your dates.

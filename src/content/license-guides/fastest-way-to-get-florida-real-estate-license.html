Getting started

The fastest legal way to get a Florida real estate license.

There is no shortcut around the state’s requirements. The time you save comes from running the steps side by side and passing the state exam the first time.

The short answer

Submit your DBPR application and fingerprints right away, then complete the required 63-hour Course 1 at your own pace while the state reviews your application. Schedule the Pearson (formerly Pearson VUE) exam as soon as you are eligible, prepare well enough to pass on the first attempt, and have a broker ready to activate your license.

Every Florida sales associate goes through the same requirements: an application, fingerprints, the prelicense course and school certification, the state exam, and activation with a broker. Most delays come from doing those steps one after another instead of together, or from needing a second exam attempt. For the full list of steps, see how to get your Florida real estate license.

Apply to DBPR first.

You do not have to finish Course 1 before you apply. Submit the sales associate application early so the state review and your coursework move forward at the same time.

DBPR instructs applicants to get fingerprinted after applying, using an FDLE-registered Livescan provider. DBPR says fingerprint results usually arrive within three to five business days after the scan. That is a transmission estimate, not a promise of approval. Missing documents or background questions are the most common reasons an application takes longer, so answer every question completely the first time.

Not sure what to gather? Use the licensing checklist.

Choose a self-paced course.

The 63-hour Course 1 is required. What varies is how the course is delivered. A self-paced online course lets you work at your own speed and take the final when you’re ready, instead of following a fixed class schedule.

With Sales FastPass at Real Estate FastPass (formerly Daniel Larson Training), you enroll in the full Course 1, and three certification exams take the place of the traditional course final. They are built to reflect the question style and subject matter of the state exam. Take them when you are ready, and pass all three with 75% or higher to earn your course completion certificate. The course, workbook, audio, and video stay available for anything you want to learn or review.

That work does double duty: it completes your school certification and prepares you for the separate state exam. See how Course 1 and FastPass work or explore Sales FastPass.

Book the exam as soon as you are eligible.

DBPR says scheduling eligibility should be available within 72 hours after your application is approved. Look for your authorization notice and candidate identification number, then choose the Sales Associate exam through Pearson’s Florida real estate page.

Appointment availability varies by testing center, so check more than one location if your first choice is booked. Print your school completion certificate before exam day; you will be turned away without it. See what to bring and how the approvals fit together.

Prepare to pass on the first attempt.

A failed exam means another fee, another appointment, and more waiting. DBPR’s own exam reports show that repeat test-takers pass at a noticeably lower rate than first-time takers. See what DBPR’s pass-rate numbers mean.

Study from the official content outline, practice explaining why each answer is right, and work the math yourself. Use the exam study plan, try Daniel’s video sample questions, and review the math examples.

Line up a broker before you pass.

A sales associate cannot practice until the license is active under an employing broker. You can research brokerages while you study, so activation does not become a new waiting period after the exam.

Ask about membership, MLS access, and start-up costs before you commit. See the questions to ask a brokerage or browse Find a Broker.

What does not make it faster.

  • Looking for a way around the required education. Course 1 is required unless DBPR confirms an exemption, such as for certain attorneys or holders of a four-year real estate degree. Ask DBPR before you rely on one.
  • Buying exam prep instead of a licensing course. Exam Prep Only includes all the explainer videos, plus optional Course 1 or optional Course 2 depending on the package, but it does not issue a course completion certificate.
  • Booking the exam before you are ready. An early appointment you do not pass usually costs more time than a later one you do.
  • Practicing before activation. Passing the exam is not the same as having an active license under a broker.

How to get your Florida real estate license.

The state’s steps, in order: who can apply, the DBPR application, fingerprints, the 63-hour course, the state exam, and activating your license with a broker.

The short answer

For the standard sales associate path, apply to DBPR, submit fingerprints, complete approved 63-hour prelicense education and your school’s certification requirements, then pass the separate state exam and activate your license with a broker. These steps are the same whichever approved school you choose.

This page covers the state’s process.

Who can apply?

You must be at least 18, have a high school diploma or equivalent, and provide a U.S. Social Security number. DBPR reviews your application and background. A school enrollment is not a state approval.

Already licensed elsewhere, an attorney, or holding a real estate degree? Ask DBPR which application route and education exemptions apply before purchasing. The ordinary Course 1 path is not the only route into Florida licensure.

How long does getting licensed take?

Your course work, application review, and exam appointment run on different schedules. You can submit the DBPR application before finishing Course 1, so the state review and your education can move forward together.

Apply, then submit fingerprints. DBPR says fingerprint results usually arrive within three to five business days after the scan. That is a transmission estimate, not a promise of application approval.

Complete your school requirements while the application is reviewed. Plan study time around your experience and schedule. The 63-hour course requirement is not a guaranteed number of calendar days to a license.

Wait for exam eligibility, then choose an available appointment. DBPR says scheduling eligibility should be available within 72 hours after application approval. That clock starts after approval; it is not a 72-hour application turnaround.

Pass the state exam and complete activation. Do not begin practicing until the required broker registration is in place.

Missing documents, a background review, your preparation, or testing-center availability can extend the process. Check DBPR’s scheduling instructions and keep track of your separate expiration dates.

After passing, the next education step is post-licensing.

Record the initial expiration date shown by DBPR. Sales post-licensing is your first-renewal education; ongoing CE comes later. Keeping that distinction clear now helps you avoid buying the wrong course next year. Read the first-renewal guide.

Joining a brokerage also raises questions about REALTOR® membership and MLS access. Those are separate from your state license. Learn the difference between a license and REALTOR® membership, then see what to ask your broker about membership and MLS access.

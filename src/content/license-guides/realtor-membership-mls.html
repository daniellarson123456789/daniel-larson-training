The short answer

Your state license, REALTOR® membership, and MLS access are three different things. Completing a licensing course does not enroll you in an association or activate an MLS account. Your brokerage, association, and MLS confirm their own requirements and charges.

Does my license make me a REALTOR®?

No. Florida licenses sales associates and brokers. A REALTOR® is a member of the National Association of REALTORS® who agrees to follow its Code of Ethics. Earning a state license does not automatically create that membership.

For someone entering as a sales associate, the state licensing process comes first. Before performing services that require a license, confirm your active status and the required employing-broker registration. Association membership does not replace those steps.

You can research brokerages and their costs while working toward your license. See the Florida sales licensing process.

Do I have to join if my broker is a REALTOR®?

Ask what the brokerage requires. A brokerage can make REALTOR® membership an office requirement, but that is different from a state licensing requirement.

NAR’s membership guidance allows affiliated licensees to choose not to join. It also provides for a nonmember assessment charged to the firm’s designated REALTOR®. That financial consequence is part of the conversation to have with your prospective broker.

For a sales associate joining as a REALTOR®, membership ordinarily begins through a local association, with state and national membership included. Your broker’s affiliation affects the available arrangements. Confirm them before submitting an application or paying dues.

Does REALTOR® membership include MLS access?

Association membership and MLS access are often arranged together, but they are separate services with their own participation, subscription, and fee rules. Joining an association does not mean that every MLS account is automatically established or paid for.

Florida also has non-REALTOR® MLS participation arrangements, commonly associated with “Thompson brokers.” Access still depends on the MLS’s requirements and your broker’s participation. This is not a universal option that a sales associate can select independently of the brokerage.

Ask which MLS the office uses, how your account will be set up, and what training or access tools you will need. Your licensing school does not activate that account.

Which classes are separate from state education?

RequirementWhat to confirm
Florida licensing, post-licensing, or CEThe education required for your license and renewal stage, through DBPR and your approved provider.
Association orientation, ethics, and fair housingYour association’s accepted courses and the deadlines applying to your membership.
MLS instructionYour MLS’s onboarding, system-training, and continuing requirements.

NAR requires Code of Ethics and Fair Housing training for REALTOR® members. Joining and orientation deadlines vary by association; do not assume one statewide “30-day rule.” Obtain the schedule from the organization you join.

A class may satisfy more than one obligation when it is appropriately approved and accepted. An “ethics” title alone does not establish that it meets both state CE and association requirements. Confirm which credit you will receive, who reports it, and whether you must submit a certificate.

First-renewal post-licensing and later-renewal CE have their own state requirements. Keep those deadlines separate from your membership and MLS calendar.

What will I pay—and to whom?

Separate the cost of getting licensed from the cost of starting work. Your education package covers its stated school services; it does not include association dues, an MLS subscription, or brokerage charges.

ExpenseWhere to confirm it
Licensing course and optional exam preparationYour school
Application, fingerprints, and state examinationDBPR and the applicable service providers
Association dues and joining chargesYour local association
MLS subscriptions and access toolsYour MLS or association
Brokerage charges and included servicesYour broker

Ask for the amount due when you start and the recurring amount afterward. Check whether charges are prorated, included elsewhere, or optional. Plan the separate expenses of getting licensed.

Questions to ask before choosing a brokerage.

  • Which association and MLS would I use?
  • Does the office require REALTOR® membership?
  • What will I pay upfront, and which charges repeat?
  • Which orientations and classes must I complete, and by when?
  • Who helps with applications and account activation?
  • Which expenses and services does the brokerage cover?

Get these answers from the office you are considering. Your school handles your course and its stated services; the broker, association, and MLS confirm the arrangements for beginning work.
